What I hold myself to

Rules

A principle is professional and general — it holds for anyone doing the work. A rule holds for one person, and is written down precisely because it would otherwise get quietly dropped. Collected here rather than written as one block: a manifesto is what these become, not what they start as. These are not slogans. A rule is written down so an action can be checked against it afterwards — by me, and by my personal assistant: the second brain, the virtual me. It reads this page and is expected to hold me to it, which is why each rule has its own page rather than living as a line in a list.

All items:

  • Build something you did not have to. — Then use it, fix it, and use it again.
  • Do not come back the same. — The only real failure condition.
  • Do the dull half first. — The paperwork, the doctor, the drawer that has not opened since 2019.
  • Getting lost will help you find yourself. — Travel often, and be willing not to know where you are.
  • If you don't like something, change it. — Not advice — a statement that the option exists.
  • Keep the receipts. — A year you cannot remember is a year you did not have.
  • Life is simple. — Two words, no explanation, and it does not soften.
  • Rest does not get squeezed. — It is a third of the work, not the reward for finishing the other two.
  • Say it out loud before it is finished. — Half of it is wrong and there is no other way to find out.
  • Stop over-analysing. — The line that is hardest to follow for anyone who thinks in systems.
  • Write it down. Don't launch it. — Ideas, offerings, drafts, half-formed income models — captured properly and then deliberately left alone.
